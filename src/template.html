TrueEarnings

AI first pass at quality of earnings

When a small business is sold, the price is a multiple of its adjusted earnings: the profit left after removing the owner's personal costs, one-time events and items booked in the wrong year. Finding those adjustments is a quality-of-earnings (QoE) review. Most small deals skip it because it costs more than they can justify. TrueEarnings reads the general ledger, proposes each adjustment with its reasoning and the document that would prove it, and leaves every decision to the analyst.

Does it work? The benchmark

Before any AI run, I planted 15 adjustments in the demo ledger and wrote an answer key, plus 13 decoys: legitimate costs that look suspicious (crew-truck leases, trade-association dues, routine legal work). The AI never sees the key. For comparison, a keyword screen searches payees and memos for the terms an analyst would filter on (settlement, personal, club, tuition, refund, gain on sale, and so on).

The ledger

What it can't do yet

  • Income statement only. It reviews profit-and-loss accounts. A full QoE also covers the balance sheet: net working capital, proof of cash against bank statements, and debt-like items.
  • Flags are hypotheses. Each adjustment lists the document that would prove or kill it. Nothing should reach a buyer until those documents are reviewed.
  • Market rates are estimates. Replacement salaries and market rent come from the model's general knowledge, stated as assumptions. A real engagement would use local comps.
  • One year, one company. The benchmark is a single synthetic company with an answer key I wrote. It shows the method works on known cases; it is not a statistical accuracy rate.
  • Data privacy. Uploaded ledgers are sent to Claude. Use anonymized data until this runs inside a firm's own environment.

Where it goes next

  • Three-year trend view, so one-time items stand out against a baseline instead of relying on memos.
  • Bank-statement upload for proof of cash: do deposits match recorded revenue?
  • Direct import from QuickBooks Online and Xero instead of CSV.
  • A seller-readiness mode: owners clean up add-backs a year before going to market, when it's cheapest.
  • A buyer mode for search-fund and SBA buyers, scoring a broker's add-back schedule against the ledger.
